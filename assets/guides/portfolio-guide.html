☆ 01
Introduction — Why Your Portfolio Matters

Your portfolio is your digital business card, your gallery, and your resume all in one. It's often the first thing potential clients, galleries, or employers will see — and it needs to make a strong impression.

"Remember to play. It's fun to have a relationship with the spirituality of it all. Everybody can be an artist, everybody can participate. It's a human right to participate in art, and it always gives back."

— Cara Romero

What Makes a Great Portfolio?

Quality Why It Matters
Curated Shows only your best work
Cohesive Has a consistent style or theme
Professional Clean design, easy to navigate
Current Up-to-date with your latest work
Accessible Fast loading, works on all devices
☆ 02
What to Include — The Essential Elements

Must-Have Sections

Section What to Include
Hero/Header Your name, title, and a strong tagline
Gallery 10-20 of your best artworks
About/Bio Your artist statement and background
Contact Email, social links, commission info
CV/Resume Experience, exhibitions, education

Nice-to-Have Sections

  • Testimonials — Quotes from clients or galleries
  • Process Work — Sketches, WIP, behind-the-scenes
  • Services — What you offer (commissions, prints, etc.)
  • Press — Articles or features about your work

☆ 04
Selecting Your Best Work — Quality Over Quantity

The 80/20 Rule

80% of the impact comes from 20% of your work. Be ruthless in your curation.

Questions to Ask Yourself

Question Why It Matters
Does this represent my best work? Only show work you're proud of
Does this fit my current style? Old work can confuse your brand
Would I put this on my wall? If not, don't include it
Does this show range? Variety is good, but stay consistent

How Many Pieces?

Stage Recommended Count
Beginner 8-12 pieces
Intermediate 12-20 pieces
Professional 15-25 pieces
💡

Pro Tip: It's better to have 10 amazing pieces than 50 mediocre ones.

☆ 05
Writing Your Artist Statement — Tell Your Story

What Is an Artist Statement?

A short paragraph that explains:

  • Who you are as an artist
  • What you create and why
  • What inspires you
  • What you hope to achieve

The Formula

[Name] is a [type of artist] based in [location].
Their work explores [themes/subjects] through [medium/technique].
Inspired by [influences], they aim to [goal/purpose].

Examples

GOOD

"Summer Lee is a digital illustrator based in Berlin. Her work explores the intersection of technology and nature, creating surreal landscapes that feel both familiar and otherworldly. Inspired by sci-fi and classical painting, she aims to transport viewers to imaginative worlds."

BETTER

"Summer Lee creates digital dreamscapes where neon meets nature. Based in Berlin, her work explores the tension between the organic and the artificial — a reflection of our increasingly digital world. Her illustrations have been featured in Wired, The Verge, and exhibited in galleries across Europe."

Tips for a Strong Statement

  • ✅ Be authentic and personal
  • ✅ Keep it concise (100-150 words)
  • ✅ Avoid jargon and clichés
  • ✅ Show, don't tell
  • ✅ Update it regularly
☆ 06
Design Tips — Make It Look Professional

Do's and Don'ts

✓ Do
  • Keep it clean and simple
  • Use consistent fonts
  • Choose a color palette
  • Make it responsive
  • Test on different devices
✗ Don't
  • Overcrowd with elements
  • Use more than 2-3 fonts
  • Use random colors
  • Ignore mobile users
  • Assume it looks good everywhere

☆ 08
Launch Checklist — Before You Go Live

Technical Check

◻ All links work (no 404s)
◻ Images load fast (check file sizes)
◻ Mobile responsive (test on phone/tablet)
◻ Cross-browser compatible (Chrome, Firefox, Safari)
◻ SSL certificate active (HTTPS)
◻ Loading time < 3 seconds

Content Check

◻ Artist statement is up-to-date
◻ Contact info is correct
◻ Social links work
◻ No typos or broken grammar
◻ Copyright/credit info included

Brand Check

◻ Consistent colors and fonts
◻ Professional email address
◻ Favicon set
◻ OG/Twitter cards set for social sharing
◻ Google Analytics installed (optional)

☆ Quotes and Inspiration: From people who were where you are

You made it congrats! These are just a few quotes I found from artists and entrepreneurs who agree on the necessity of a portfolio. Take some time to read through and read up on the people behind them when you can. They inspired me, I hope they can inspire you.

1. Curating Your Best Work

"Don't play it safe. Enter what you are proud of. Enter your weirdest stuff."

— Larry Buchanan (YG12), Journalist at The New York Times

"Your portfolio is only as strong as your weakest piece – so try and keep the same standard across all your work – sometimes less can be more. Add as much breadth and diversity of thinking as you can – you don't want to come across as a one-trick pony."

— Alison Hingle (YG5), Senior Creative at Joe Public United

"Six is a beautiful number - not too much and not too little. Six entries can speak for you and your work, but also offer a lot of wonder and thought for the viewers on the other side."

— Paul Buckley (YG1), Designer at Paul Buckley Design

"Self-curating your work can be stressful... For me, it's not about a single piece, but about the whole constellation of projects you will present."

— Giada Bossi (YG21), Director at Bossi Giada

2. Quality Over Quantity

"A portfolio with 10 amazing pieces will impress more than a portfolio with 50 average pieces."

— Fabian Warislohner, Game Artist

"Quality is more important than quantity, because it shows your level of skill, professionalism, and passion."

"Your portfolio needs to be focused and cohesive. A few powerful pieces are better than a large, unfocused collection."

— Carolyn Edlund, Artsy Shark

"A good rule of thumb is to focus on about 15–20 strong works that show the range within your signature style."

3. Be Authentic

"Don't let society or anyone, your mom, tell you that you cannot be an artist. Because when you don't live in your authentic self, you die."

— Ifé Franklin, Mixed-Media Artist

"Share the work you believe in most, not what you think others want to see. That kind of honest work is the clearest expression of your voice and frequency. The best portfolios aren't perfect, but they reveal passion and presence behind the craft."

— Eden Yeung (YG18), Creative Director at Spotify

"Authenticity is key to distinguishing yourself in any competitive market. The portfolio, after all, is a story about you."

— Sotheby's Institute of Art

"Don't be afraid to include some wildcards that you feel could present you in a different light and showcase a rare aspect of your creativity."

— Giada Bossi (YG21)

"Make work that truly reflects your interests and taste. Avoid chasing trends; they rarely age well. Your lived experience is your greatest asset."

— Garrett Morin (YG6), Creative Director at G.E.O.

"Keep on keeping on" — someone, probably.
