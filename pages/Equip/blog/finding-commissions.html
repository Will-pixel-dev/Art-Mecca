Finding Commissions: Where to Start

Art Mecca Team February 5, 2026 9 min read
🤝

Commission work is one of the most reliable ways for artists to earn income. But finding those first few commissions — and building a steady stream of clients — can feel overwhelming. This guide breaks down where to find commission opportunities and how to land them.

Where to Find Commissions

There are more places to find commission work than ever before. Here are the most effective channels:

  • Freelance platforms: Fiverr, Upwork, Freelancer — good for starting out, but competitive.
  • Art-specific platforms: ArtStation, Behance, DeviantArt — where clients actively look for artists.
  • Social media: Instagram, Twitter/X, TikTok — share your work and let clients come to you.
  • Job boards: Indeed, LinkedIn, We Work Remotely — for more formal employment or freelance gigs.
  • Referrals: Word of mouth is powerful. Happy clients will bring you more work.

The key is to be present where your target clients are looking.

How to Get Noticed

Even the best artists can be invisible if they don't know how to market themselves. Here's how to stand out:

  • Build a portfolio: Show your best work, clearly organized by style or type of work.
  • Be specific: Make it clear what you offer — "I create fantasy character illustrations" is better than "I make art."
  • Add a call to action: "Commissions open" or "Contact me for rates" makes it easy for clients to take the next step.
  • Post consistently: Regular activity signals that you're active and professional.

Pricing Your Commission Work

Pricing is one of the most stressful parts of commission work. Here's a simple framework:

  • Hourly rate + materials: Calculate how many hours a piece takes and multiply by your desired hourly rate.
  • Fixed rate: Set a price for common project types (e.g., $200 for a portrait).
  • Value-based pricing: Consider the value the artwork brings to the client (commercial use, emotional impact).

Don't forget to factor in revisions — decide how many are included and what you'll charge for extras.

Building a Professional Process

A smooth process builds trust and leads to repeat clients. Here's a commission workflow to adopt:

  • Initial consultation: Understand the client's needs, style preferences, and budget.
  • Contract: Scope of work, timeline, payment schedule, and revision policy.
  • Deposit: Require 50% upfront to confirm the booking.
  • Updates: Send WIP images at key stages for client feedback.
  • Final delivery: Send high-resolution files and require final payment before handover.
  • Follow-up: Thank the client and ask for a testimonial or referral.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Here are some pitfalls that can kill your commission business:

  • Underpricing: It devalues your work and attracts difficult clients.
  • No contract: Without a contract, you have no recourse if things go wrong.
  • Scope creep: Don't let clients add extra work for free. Stick to the agreed scope.
  • Burning out: Take on only as much work as you can handle while maintaining quality.
  • Neglecting marketing: Even with a full schedule, you need to keep your pipeline active for the future.

Building Long-Term Relationships

One-off commissions are great, but repeat clients are where the real stability lies.

  • Deliver quality: Always exceed expectations.
  • Be reliable: Meet deadlines and communicate proactively.
  • Stay in touch: Send a holiday email, share new work, or offer returning client discounts.
